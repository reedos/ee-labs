REED’s Engineering Labs

Electrical Engineering focused.

Four interactive labs for undergraduate circuits, signals and control. Wire a circuit from its elements and read the meters, build a signal and filter it, read a circuit's poles, or close a loop and measure its margins. A circuit built in one lab opens in the others as the same system: an RLC wired in Circuit Elements Lab opens in Circuit Lab, and from there as a filter in Signal Lab and a plant in Control Lab. Every number and every statement on screen is checked by a test against what the page draws.

⏚ΩCircuit Elements Lab

Circuits I, on a schematic with live meters: the two laws, then the theorems, then the differential equations, then phasors, and out into Circuit Lab. Every reading is a solved node voltage, and each lesson asks you to predict the number before you turn the knob.

58 experiments, 9 groups

~ΣSignal Lab

Build waveforms from harmonics, sample them, and run them through chains of biquads and FIR filters. Each result is shown as a spectrum, an impulse response, a z-plane plot and a group-delay curve. A convolution view steps through the sum one sample at a time.

35 experiments

⎓⏦Circuit Lab

RC, RLC, twin-T and op-amp stages. Component values set the poles, the Bode plot and the step response. Each H(s) is derived on screen, and a ±5% tolerance view shows how far the response moves.

16 experiments, 10 circuits

⊕→Control Lab

Close a feedback loop and measure it: gain and phase margins, root locus, Nyquist, disturbance rejection, and S + T = 1. Seven plants and four controllers. Each rule of thumb states the conditions under which it holds.

13 experiments, 7 plants

One circuit, three labs

A series RLC in Circuit Lab is also a biquad. Hand it to Signal Lab and the same f₀ and Q filter a signal, exactly at the pre-warped corner. The same network is also a second-order plant. Hand it to Control Lab and ask how much gain the loop takes before it oscillates. The three labs read one transfer function three ways, and where a mapping cannot be exact, the panel says so and gives the reason.
