# RF Lab: the plan

Tier 5 of `ANALOG_ROADMAP.md`, and the top of track A in `EE_LABS_MAP.md`. The radio
front end, where impedance is matched rather than driven, and where the transistor is
used near its `f_T`. Splash glyph `≋`, directory `apps/rf-lab`, engine as a new
package `packages/rf`.

The path, in order. The line at one frequency, and the reflection it sends back. The
Smith chart as the map of that one number. Matching networks in closed form.
S-parameters as the two-port description the industry measures. The transistor near
`f_T`, its stability and its available gain. Noise figure and the low-noise amplifier.
The mixer, the image, and the two-tone test. The oscillator's phase noise, and the
power amplifier's classes.

This is a draft (2026-09-05) for Reed to settle. The lab's two dependencies are not
built. The Fields Lab is being built on `lab/fields-lab` and owns the transmission
line's first half. The Analog IC Lab is mapped and owns the Gilbert cell. §1 says
exactly what this lab leans on from each, and §9 phases the build so that the parts
with no dependency ship first.

The two rules that govern the other labs govern this one with no exemption. **Every
explanatory sentence is a claim about physics, and a test must measure it.** And
`CORE_SCOPE.md` decides what the engine may state exactly, what it may approximate
behind a guard, and what it declines with a reason. This lab is where the scope
document's own worked refusal becomes a lesson. The transmission line has no rational
H(s), it has an exact complex two-port at every frequency, and the difference between
those two facts is the content of Group A.

## 0. Open decisions

### Decision 1: the name (recommended: RF Lab)

`ANALOG_ROADMAP.md` §2 and `EE_LABS_MAP.md` §1 both call tier 5 the RF Lab, and the
course it mirrors is called RF circuit design in most catalogues. LabNav short form
**"RF"**. The splash card names the path in one line: "the line, the Smith chart,
matching, S-parameters, the low-noise amplifier, the mixer, the oscillator".

Alternatives considered. *Microwave Lab* names the frequency band rather than the
circuits, and this lab starts at 100 MHz. *Radio Lab* promises modulation and
demodulation, which are the Communications Lab's. *High-Frequency Lab* names a
condition rather than a subject.

### Decision 2: where the budget arithmetic lives

The System Lab's cascaded noise figure and cascaded IP3 are the same formulas this
lab uses in Groups F and G. Recommended: **`packages/rf/src/budget.js`**, written by
this lab's overseer with the System Lab named as its second user in the contract.
The alternative is a `packages/budget` of its own, which would hold four functions
and import `rf`'s block record anyway.

The consequence is that the System Lab depends on a package this overseer owns. The
director resolves it the way `PROGRAM.md` §5 resolves every shared package. A change
requested by the System Lab is written into that lab's `NEEDS.md` and landed here.

### Decision 3: how much of the line this lab owns

The Fields Lab's second half covers the telegrapher's equations, the characteristic
impedance, the bounce diagram and the lossless line in time. This lab needs the line
as a **two-port at one frequency** and nothing before that. Recommended: **the Fields
Lab derives, this lab evaluates**. `packages/rf/src/line.js` holds the exact complex
two-port and cites the Fields Lab's group for where `Z_0` and `γ` come from.

If the Fields Lab is not released when this lab is, Group A defines `Z_0` and `γ` in
a term panel. The cross-reference stays unlinked until that group ships. The
progression test enforces it, because a note may not name an experiment that does not
exist.

### Decision 4: which transistor the device groups use

Groups E to H need S-parameters for a real device. Recommended: **two curated
S-parameter sets shipped as data, both derived from a small-signal netlist this
suite can solve.** The set is generated by `smallSignal` and `transferOf` from the
Electronics Lab's hybrid-π at a stated operating point, converted to S by
`packages/rf`. Nothing is loaded from a vendor file, which keeps `EE_LABS_MAP.md` §5's
rule about the bench.

The two sets are the ones §4.3 names. One is potentially unstable at 2.000 GHz with
`K = 0.607`. One is unconditionally stable at 8.000 GHz with `K = 1.433`. A lab that
teaches stability circles needs both cases on screen.

### Decision 5: whether the Smith chart goes into `packages/ui`

`PROGRAM.md` §4 already answers this. The Smith chart's first lab is the RF Lab, and
its second and third are the Fields Lab and the Instruments Lab. Recommended:
**`packages/ui/src/SmithCanvas.jsx` from the first commit**, with the props the other
two labs need carried from the start. §4.2 lists those props. The S-parameter view is
the same decision with the same two second users.

## 2. The engine: `packages/rf`, exact at every frequency

### 2.1 What exists, and what is missing

missing is listed here, and nothing else is built.

### 2.2 The scope stance, object by object

`CORE_SCOPE.md` is answered here once, for every object the package holds. Each row
says admitted, guarded or declined, and the reason. §2.13's invariants test each row.

| Object | Stance | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The S-matrix of a lumped linear circuit at one frequency | admitted, exact | a complex matrix from the exact AC solve, with no approximation between them |
| S to Z, Y and ABCD, and back | admitted, exact | a bilinear map on matrices, invertible wherever the inverse exists |
| The Smith chart's circles | admitted, exact | the image of a line or circle under a Möbius map is a line or circle |
| Cascading two-ports | admitted, exact | one matrix product for ABCD, one closed form for S |
| K, μ, Δ, the stability circles | admitted, exact | algebraic functions of the four S entries |

| The unilateral approximation | guarded | the figure of merit U bounds the error, and the bound is printed and tested |
| The lossless or lossy line as a two-port at ω | admitted, exact per frequency | `cosh γl` and `sinh γl` evaluated in complex arithmetic |
| The line as a rational H(s) | **declined** | `e^{−γl}` is transcendental and has no finite poles or zeros |

Leeson's phase noise is the one object here that a reader could mistake for physics.
It is a **model**, its flicker corner `f_c` is a fitted number, and the pane that
draws it says so beside the curve. §2.12 states the label.

### 2.3 S-parameters as the wave description

`sparam.js` defines a two-port by its scattering matrix at a reference impedance. The
record is `{ f, z0, s: [[S11, S12], [S21, S22]] }` with each entry a `complex.js`
pair. A one-port is the same record with a single entry.

The definition the lab teaches is the one the package implements. The incident and
reflected waves at port k are `a_k = (V_k + Z_0 I_k)/(2√Z_0)` and
`b_k = (V_k − Z_0 I_k)/(2√Z_0)`, and `S` is the matrix with `b = S a`. Every entry is
measured with the other port terminated in `Z_0`, which is why the description works
at a frequency where an open circuit is not open.

`sFromNetlist(net, ports, f)` builds the S-matrix of any circuit `packages/network`
can solve. It drives port 1 with a source of internal resistance `Z_0`, terminates
port 2 in `Z_0`, solves once with `solveAC`, and reads the two waves. Then it swaps
the ports and solves again. Two exact AC solves give four exact complex numbers.

### 2.4 Conversion, exact and round-tripped

`convert.js` holds six functions, each a closed form on 2 by 2 complex matrices.
`sToZ`, `zToS`, `sToY`, `yToS`, `sToAbcd` and `abcdToS`. The reference form is
`S = (Z − Z_0 I)(Z + Z_0 I)^{-1}`, with the inverse computed from the determinant.

Every conversion is exact where the inverse exists. Where it does not, the function
throws with the reason the singular matrix gives, in the register `SingularError`
already uses in `linalg.js`. An ideal transformer has no Z-matrix, its S-matrix is
finite, and the lab shows that case rather than hiding it. Invariant 1 requires the
S to Z to ABCD to Y to S round trip to hold to `1e-12` relative.

### 2.5 The Smith chart is a Möbius map

`smith.js` holds the geometry as numbers, not as a picture. The chart is the map
`Γ = (z − 1)/(z + 1)` from the normalised impedance plane to the unit disc. The two
families of curves come out of that map in closed form.

A constant-resistance circle `r` maps to the circle centred at `(r/(1 + r), 0)` with
radius `1/(1 + r)`. A constant-reactance circle `x` maps to the circle centred at
`(1, 1/x)` with radius `1/|x|`. Both are exact, and both are computed rather than
drawn from a table. The admittance chart is the same picture rotated by 180 degrees,
which is the map `Γ_y = −Γ_z`.

Three further families the app draws. A constant-`|Γ|` circle is a VSWR circle,
centred at the origin with radius `(S − 1)/(S + 1)`. A constant-Q arc is the circle
through `±1` centred at `(0, ±1/Q)` with radius `√(1 + 1/Q²)`. A noise circle and a
gain circle come from `stability.js` and `noise.js` as a centre and a radius.

### 2.6 Cascading

`cascade.js` composes two-ports two ways, and the lab uses both. The ABCD form is a
matrix product, and it is the one to use for a chain of series and shunt elements.
The S form is the closed composition

```
S11 = A11 + A12 B11 A21 / (1 − A22 B11)
S12 = A12 B12 / (1 − A22 B11)
S21 = A21 B21 / (1 − A22 B11)
S22 = B22 + B21 A22 B12 / (1 − A22 B11)
```

with `A` the left two-port and `B` the right. The two routes agree to floating point,
which is invariant 2. ABCD composes by multiplication and needs no reference
impedance. S is what a network analyser reports. The composition has a singular case
at `A22 B11 = 1`, a lossless resonance between two mismatched ports, and the function
throws with that reason.

### 2.7 Stability and gain, as circles on the chart

`stability.js` computes, from one S-matrix, the numbers a designer reads before
anything else. `Δ = S11 S22 − S12 S21`. Rollett's factor
`K = (1 − |S11|² − |S22|² + |Δ|²) / (2|S12 S21|)`. The geometric factor
`μ = (1 − |S11|²) / (|S22 − Δ S11*| + |S12 S21|)`, which is a single sufficient test.

The load stability circle has centre `(S22 − Δ S11*)* / (|S22|² − |Δ|²)` and radius
`|S12 S21| / | |S22|² − |Δ|² |`. The source circle is the same expression with the
indices swapped. Both are exact, and the pane shades the unstable region rather than
naming it.

When `K > 1` and `|Δ| < 1` the device is unconditionally stable, and
`MAG = |S21/S12| (K − √(K² − 1))` is reached at the simultaneous conjugate match. The
closed form for `Γ_S` and `Γ_L` is Pozar's, and it is exact. When `K ≤ 1` the pane
prints `MSG = |S21/S12|` instead, and states that a stabilising element is needed
before any match is meaningful.

### 2.8 The line: exact at every frequency, and no H(s)

`line.js` is the object `CORE_SCOPE.md` names in its worked-example table. A uniform
line of characteristic impedance `Z_0`, propagation constant `γ = α + jβ` and length
`l` has the exact ABCD matrix

```
A = cosh(γ l)      B = Z_0 sinh(γ l)
C = sinh(γ l)/Z_0  D = cosh(γ l)
```

Every entry is a complex number at the frequency asked for, and every one is computed
in complex arithmetic with no series truncation. The input impedance of a loaded line
follows, `Z_in = Z_0 (Z_L + Z_0 tanh γl) / (Z_0 + Z_L tanh γl)`, and it is exact.

What the package does not provide is a rational H(s) for the line, and the pane says
why. `e^{−γl}` has no finite poles and no finite zeros, so no ratio of polynomials
equals it. The hand-over to `@ee-labs/systems` is declined at the boundary with that
sentence, and the refusal has a test like every other claim. A Padé model would be a
new labelled object under Rule 3, and no experiment in this lab needs one. The sweep
stays exact point by point. A reader who asks for 241 frequencies gets 241 exact
answers, and a reader who asks for a pole gets the reason there is not one.

### 2.9 Matching networks in closed form

`match.js` synthesises rather than searches. For a resistive load `R_L` and a source
`R_S` with `R_L > R_S`, the L network that matches them has

```
Q  = √(R_L/R_S − 1)
X_series  = Q R_S       (in the low-impedance branch)
X_parallel = R_L / Q    (across the high-impedance branch)
```

and the two element values follow from `X = ωL` or `X = 1/(ωC)`. There are four
topologies, two orientations by two reactance signs, and the function returns all
four with the one the panel shows named. The loaded Q is the same `Q`, so the
fractional bandwidth is `1/Q`, which is the trade the lab makes visible.

A complex load is absorbed first. The load's reactance is combined with the nearest
element, or cancelled by a series or shunt reactance, and the residue is the
resistive case above. A three-element network (pi or T) adds one degree of freedom,
which the panel exposes as the chosen intermediate resistance and its Q. Synthesis is
exact, and invariant 4 requires `|Γ| < 1e-12` at the design frequency for every case
the fuzzer generates.

### 2.10 Noise

The Electronics Lab's `noise.js` gives spectral densities from thermal and shot
sources through an exactly solved network. This lab adds the two-port description
that a front-end designer uses, and the cascade arithmetic.

Noise figure is `F = SNR_in / SNR_out` at a stated source temperature of 290 K. A
matched passive block's noise figure equals its loss, which the lab derives rather
than asserts. Friis's cascade formula is
`F = F_1 + (F_2 − 1)/G_1 + (F_3 − 1)/(G_1 G_2) + …`, exact for available gains
between matched stages.

The two-port form is `F = F_min + (R_n/G_S) |Y_S − Y_opt|²`, with four real numbers
describing the device. Constant-F contours are circles on the source plane, and
`noiseCircle(F)` returns the centre and radius in closed form. The lab draws them on
the same Smith chart as the gain circles, because the trade between them is the
low-noise amplifier's whole design problem.

### 2.11 Linearity, measured by the FFT that exists

`linearity.js` does not assume a polynomial. It drives a block with two tones, takes
the spectrum with `packages/dsp`'s `fft`, and reads the fundamental and the
third-order products at `2f_1 − f_2` and `2f_2 − f_1`. The tones are chosen to land
on exact bins, so no window is needed and no leakage enters the reading.

`OIP3 = P_out + Δ/2` where `Δ` is the decibel gap between a fundamental and its
adjacent third-order product. `IIP3 = OIP3 − G`. The formula is a third-order
extrapolation, and it is exact only in the limit of small drive. §4.3 gives the
measured error at four drive levels, and the guard follows from it.

**The guard.** The pane warns when the drive is within 10 dB of the block's own 1 dB
compression point, and declines to report IP3 when it is within 3 dB of it. At the
warning threshold the extrapolation error is under 0.05 dB. At the declining
threshold it is over 0.15 dB and rising, and the number would be read as a
measurement rather than as an extrapolation.

Cascaded input IP3 uses `1/IIP3 = 1/IIP3_1 + G_1/IIP3_2 + G_1 G_2/IIP3_3 + …` in
linear power. The formula assumes the products add in voltage with aligned phase,
which is the worst case, and the panel says so. `budget.js` holds it, and the System
Lab is its second user.

### 2.12 Phase noise: Leeson's model, labelled

`leeson.js` implements

```
L(Δf) = 10 log [ (2 F k T / P_s) (1 + (f_0 / (2 Q_L Δf))²) (1 + f_c/Δf) ]
```

and every panel that draws it carries the label "Leeson's model" beside the curve.
The label states the three things a reader needs. `F` and `f_c` are fitted numbers
rather than derived ones. The shape is right and the absolute level depends on the
fit. The `1/Δf³`, `1/Δf²` and flat regions meet at corners the model puts at `f_c`
and at `f_0/(2 Q_L)`.

Rule 3 usually guards an approximation of an exact object. There is no exact object
here to approximate, so the guard is a label rather than a threshold.
`REVIEW_PLAYBOOK.md`'s class for labelled models reviews it before release.

### 2.13 Invariants, the fuzzer's checklist

Across random passive networks, random terminations and the two curated device sets:

1. **Conversion round-trips.** S to Z to ABCD to Y to S returns the input to `1e-12`
   relative, at every frequency in the sweep, for every network with a non-singular
   path.
2. **Two cascade routes agree.** The ABCD product and the S composition give the same
   two-port to floating point.
3. **Passivity and reciprocity.** A network of R, L and C has `S12 = S21` to floating
   point, and the largest singular value of S is at most 1. A lossless network has
   `S†S = I` to `1e-12`.
4. **A synthesised match is matched.** Every L network `match.js` returns gives
   `|Γ| < 1e-12` at its design frequency when solved by `solveAC`.
5. **The chart is the algebra.** Every point `smith.js` places is `(z − 1)/(z + 1)`
   evaluated directly, and every circle it returns contains the points it claims to,
   to `1e-12`.
6. **The line agrees with the network.** A line split into N cascaded sections of
   length `l/N`, each an exact two-port, equals the single section of length `l` to
   floating point for every N.
7. **The line is not rational.** The hand-over to `systems` is declined, the message
   names the transcendental factor, and the test asserts the refusal at both a
   lossless and a lossy line.
8. **Stability is consistent.** `K > 1` with `|Δ| < 1` holds if and only if `μ > 1`,
   and both hold if and only if neither stability circle intersects the unit disc,
   checked numerically over the disc.
9. **Gain closes.** The transducer gain computed from S and the two reflection
   coefficients equals the power ratio read from a direct AC solve of the terminated
   network, to `1e-9` relative.
10. **The unilateral bound holds.** The exact transducer gain at the unilateral
    optimum lies inside `1/(1 + U)²` and `1/(1 − U)²` of the unilateral estimate, for
    every device set and every frequency.
11. **Friis closes.** The cascaded noise figure from `budget.js` equals the output
    noise density from `noise.js` over an explicitly built cascade, to `1e-9`
    relative.
12. **IP3 closes.** The IP3 that `linearity.js` extracts from the FFT equals the
    closed form `√(4a_1/3|a_3|)` for a cubic block, within the guard's stated error at
    each drive level.
13. **Cross-lab.** The lumped LC matching network's S21 sent to Circuit Lab as H(s)
    agrees at every swept frequency. A stage's small-signal netlist from the
    Electronics Lab converted to S and back gives the same `sweepAC` response.

## 3. Models: the element library

Everything `packages/network` stamps stays. These are added, and each is a record
rather than a new stamp, because every one of them is evaluated at a frequency.

| Element | Ideal law | Non-ideality toggles (each labelled) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uniform line | `Z_0`, `γ = α + jβ`, length `l`, exact ABCD per frequency | dielectric constant sets `v_p`. Attenuation `α` in dB/m. Dispersion as `α(f) ∝ √f` for skin effect, labelled |
| Two-port from data | an S-matrix per frequency, interpolated between points | the interpolation is linear in real and imaginary parts, and the pane says so |
| Ideal transformer | turns ratio `n`, no Z-matrix, exact S-matrix | none |
| Lumped inductor | `jωL` | series `R_s` from a stated Q, parallel `C_p` giving a self-resonance |
| Lumped capacitor | `1/(jωC)` | series `R_s` and `L_s` giving a self-resonance |
| Varactor | `C_j(v)` from the Electronics Lab's `junction.js` | a small-signal value at the bias point, as `junction.js` states |
| Device from a small-signal netlist | hybrid-π at a stated point, converted to S | the operating point is on the label, as the Electronics Lab requires |
| Mixer | ideal multiplier, or a switching mixer with a square LO | conversion gain, LO leakage, gain and phase imbalance for the image ratio |
| Nonlinear block | `y = a_1 x + a_3 x³`, memoryless | `a_2` for second order, a soft limiter as the alternative shape |

**Schematic description.** As every other lab, each library circuit is a netlist with
grid positions, drawn by `packages/ui/Schematic.jsx`. Three symbols are added. A line
segment with its electrical length printed, a two-port box with its S-matrix in a
tooltip, and a mixer's circled cross. Each carries the same live-meter slots.

## 4. The app

### 4.1 Layout

The Electronics Lab's shape, unchanged. Sidebar with LabNav, the report link, the
experiment groups, the circuit picker, component NumFields with chips, model and
toggle switches, and the math panel. Main area with topbar meters, the schematic
always visible, and one pane below with a pane selector. Phone-width first, with no
horizontal scroll at 390 px, harness-checked.

The topbar shows the frequency first, then the experiment's headline numbers. Those
are `|Γ|`, VSWR, `Z_in`, `|S21|` in dB, NF, `K` and IIP3, whichever the experiment
uses. The reference impedance sits beside them, because every S number depends on it.

### 4.2 Views

- **Smith chart.** New, and the lab's signature view. It goes into `packages/ui` as
  `SmithCanvas.jsx` with the Fields Lab and the Instruments Lab as its second and
  third users. Five props are fixed from the first commit. `mode` selects impedance,
  admittance or the overlaid pair. `points` carries labelled markers, and `paths`
  carries motion along a line or through a matching network. `circles` carries the
  VSWR, Q, stability, gain and noise families. `normalise` sets the reference
  impedance.
- **S-parameter view.** New, and the second shared canvas. Four magnitude traces in
  dB against frequency with phase on a second axis, a marker that reads all four
  entries at one frequency, and a toggle to a polar plot of any one entry. The
  Instruments Lab's network analyser group is its second user, and its props carry a
  calibration-plane offset from the start.
- **Schematic with the line drawn to scale.** The line's physical length is drawn
  against the wavelength at the current frequency, so a quarter-wave section looks
  like one and stops looking like one when the frequency moves.
- **Sweep.** `|Γ|`, VSWR, `Z_in` or any S entry against frequency, with the exact
  points marked. This is the pane that carries §2.8's message, and the message sits
  under the plot rather than in a tooltip.
- **Spectrum.** The two-tone output from the lab's own FFT, with the fundamentals and
  the third-order products marked and the gap `Δ` printed. Signal Lab's Nonlinearity
  group is cross-referenced by name here.
- **IP3 plot.** Output power against input power on log axes, the fundamental at slope
  1 and the product at slope 3, and their extrapolated intersection. The measured
  points stop where the guard stops, and the extrapolation is drawn dashed past them.
- **Noise view.** The noise circles and gain circles on the Smith chart, with the
  chosen source reflection marked and the resulting NF and gain printed.
- **Phase noise.** `L(Δf)` on log axes with the three regions labelled and the two
  corners marked, and the label naming Leeson's model beside the curve.
- **Equations.** The S-matrix printed as four complex numbers, then the conversion the
  experiment is using, in the way the Elements lab prints its MNA rows.

### 4.3 Numbers

The defaults are chosen so that every quoted number is round enough to remember and
every picture fits a phone. All were computed before they were written here.

- Reference impedance `Z_0 = 50 Ω` throughout. A 100 Ω load gives `Γ = 0.3333`,
  VSWR 2.000 and 9.542 dB of return loss. A 25 Ω load gives the same VSWR at
  `Γ = −0.3333`. A load of `30 − j40 Ω` gives `Γ = −j0.5000`, VSWR 3.000 and 6.021 dB
  of return loss.
- The line: PTFE with `ε_r = 2.1`, so `v_p = 2.0688 × 10⁸ m/s`, 69.007 % of `c`. At
  1.000 GHz the wavelength is 20.688 cm and a quarter wave is 5.1719 cm. At 2.400 GHz
  the wavelength is 8.6199 cm.
- That quarter-wave section of 50 Ω line loaded with 100 Ω gives `Z_in = 25.000 Ω` at
  1.000 GHz, `40.000 − j30.000 Ω` at 500 MHz, and `100.000 Ω` at 2.000 GHz, with
  `|Γ| = 0.3333` at all three. With `α = 0.05 Np/m`, which is 0.4343 dB/m, the
  1.000 GHz value moves to `25.097 Ω`.
- The quarter-wave transformer from 50 Ω to 100 Ω needs `Z_0 = 70.711 Ω`. Its
  fractional bandwidth to VSWR 1.2222 is 36.700 %.
- The L network from 50 Ω to 100 Ω at 1.000 GHz: `Q = 1.0000`, a series 7.9577 nH and
  a shunt 1.5915 pF, and a fractional bandwidth of 100.00 %. From 5 Ω to 50 Ω:
  `Q = 3.0000`, 2.3873 nH and 9.5493 pF, and 33.333 %. The 50 Ω to 100 Ω match reads
  VSWR 1.1437 at 900 MHz and 2.3570 at 1.500 GHz.
- The 3.000 dB pi attenuator in 50 Ω is a 17.615 Ω series resistor between two
  292.40 Ω shunt resistors. Its S11 is zero and its S21 is 0.70795, which is
  −3.0000 dB. Two of them cascade to −6.0000 dB with S11 still zero.
- Device set 1, potentially unstable at 2.000 GHz. `S11 = 0.894 ∠ −60.6°`,
  `S12 = 0.020 ∠ 62.4°`, `S21 = 3.122 ∠ 123.6°`, `S22 = 0.781 ∠ −27.6°`. So
  `|Δ| = 0.6964`, `K = 0.6071`, `μ = 0.8628`, and `MSG = 21.934 dB`. The load
  stability circle has centre `0.9347 + j0.9914` and radius 0.4997.
- Device set 2, unconditionally stable at 8.000 GHz. `S11 = 0.641 ∠ 171.3°`,
  `S12 = 0.057 ∠ 16.3°`, `S21 = 2.058 ∠ 28.5°`, `S22 = 0.572 ∠ −95.7°`. So
  `|Δ| = 0.2726`, `K = 1.4332`, `μ = 1.1314`, and `MAG = 11.667 dB` at
  `Γ_S = 0.7840 ∠ −167.28°` and `Γ_L = 0.7451 ∠ 101.18°`, which are `6.13 − j5.49 Ω`
  and `12.06 + j39.64 Ω`.
- The unilateral bound on device set 2: `U = 0.10851`, so the error lies between
  −0.895 dB and +0.998 dB. The measured error is +0.834 dB, since `G_TU` is 10.288 dB
  and the exact `G_T` is 11.122 dB at the same terminations.
- Noise: `k T_0 = −173.975 dBm/Hz` at 290 K. An LNA at 1.5 dB has an equivalent
  temperature of 119.64 K. A 15 dB LNA at 1.5 dB, a 10 dB mixer at 6 dB and a 20 dB IF
  amplifier at 10 dB give `NF = 1.8618 dB`. The LNA is 77.07 % of that. A 3.000 dB
  cable in front makes it 4.8618 dB.
- IP3: a cubic block with `a_1 = 1` and `a_3 = −0.1 V⁻²` has an IIP3 of 3.6515 V
  peak. That is +21.249 dBm in 50 Ω, with 1 dB compression 9.636 dB below it. The FFT
  reads the two-tone gap as 74.535 dB at −16.021 dBm and 38.098 dB at +2.041 dBm. The
  IIP3 errors are −0.0024 dB and −0.159 dB.
- The resonator: 10.000 nH at 1.000 GHz has `X_L = 62.832 Ω` and needs 2.5330 pF. At
  `Q = 20` its parallel resistance is 1256.6 Ω and its bandwidth is 50.000 MHz.
- Leeson at `f_0 = 1.000 GHz`, `Q_L = 20`, `P_s = 0 dBm`, `F = 3 dB` and
  `f_c = 100 kHz`: −89.59 dBc/Hz at 10 kHz, −117.00 dBc/Hz at 100 kHz and
  −139.59 dBc/Hz at 1.000 MHz. Doubling `Q_L` moves the 100 kHz value by 6.020 dB.
- The mixer: an ideal switching mixer has a conversion gain of `2/π`, which is
  −3.9224 dB. An ideal multiplier with a unit LO gives −6.0206 dB. At an RF of
  2.400 GHz and an LO of 2.000 GHz the IF is 400.0 MHz and the image is 1.600 GHz.
  A 1 % gain error with a 3 degree phase error gives 31.485 dB of image rejection.
- Power amplifiers: class A reaches 50.0 %, class B reaches `π/4`, which is 78.540 %,
  and a 90 degree conduction angle reaches 94.038 %. A class A stage backed off
  6.000 dB reaches 12.500 %.

## 5. Curriculum: 35 experiments in 8 groups

Format, as the other plans: **the claim** the note makes, what the reader turns, and
what is **measured** against what **formula**. Every quoted number becomes a pinned
test. The order is the progression map's. Each experiment ships with `see`, `try` and
`why` in the three registers, within the `STYLE.md` budgets.

### Group A: The line at one frequency (5)

The lab opens on the object `CORE_SCOPE.md` uses as its worked refusal. A reader
arrives from the Fields Lab with `Z_0` and `γ` and leaves with the reason a
transmission line has no transfer function.

- **A1 · The reflection coefficient.** A 50 Ω line into a 100 Ω load sends back
  `Γ = (Z_L − Z_0)/(Z_L + Z_0) = 0.3333`. A 25 Ω load sends back the same magnitude
  with the opposite sign. Turn `Z_L` and watch `Γ` trace the real axis. Measured:
  `Γ` at five loads, against the formula.
- **A2 · VSWR and return loss.** The same one number in three costumes. `Γ = 0.3333`
  is VSWR 2.000, 9.542 dB of return loss and 0.5115 dB of mismatch loss. A complex
  load of `30 − j40 Ω` gives `|Γ| = 0.5000` and VSWR 3.000. Measured: all three
  quantities at four loads.
- **A3 · The line transforms impedance.** A quarter wave of 50 Ω line turns 100 Ω into
  25 Ω, exactly, at 1.000 GHz. At 500 MHz the same section reads `40.000 − j30.000 Ω`,
  and at 2.000 GHz it reads back 100 Ω. Measured: `Z_in` at five frequencies against
  `Z_0 (Z_L + jZ_0 tan βl)/(Z_0 + jZ_L tan βl)`.
- **A4 · Loss on the line.** Set `α = 0.05 Np/m`, which is 0.4343 dB/m. The quarter
  wave's 25.000 Ω becomes 25.097 Ω and `|Γ|` at the source falls below `|Γ|` at the
  load. The two-port is still exact, since `cosh` and `sinh` take a complex argument.
  Measured: both impedances, and the round-trip loss.
- **A5 · Why the line has no transfer function.** The sweep is exact at every one of
  241 frequencies. The hand-over to Circuit Lab is declined, and the pane gives the
  reason. `e^{−γl}` is transcendental, so no ratio of polynomials equals it, and no
  finite set of poles describes it. Measured: the refusal fires, and the swept
  response matches the closed form at every point.

### Group B: The Smith chart (4)

- **B1 · The chart is one map.** `Γ = (z − 1)/(z + 1)` sends the right half plane to
  the unit disc. Drag a point in the impedance plane and watch it move in the disc.
  The open circuit is at `Γ = 1`, the short at `Γ = −1`, and the match at the centre.
  Measured: ten points mapped, against the formula.
- **B2 · Circles from the map.** Constant resistance `r = 1` is the circle centred at
  `(0.5000, 0)` with radius 0.5000. Constant reactance `x = 1` is centred at
  `(1, 1.0000)` with radius 1.0000. Both come out of the map, and the app computes
  them rather than drawing a chart image. Measured: four circles' centres and radii.
- **B3 · Motion along the line.** Adding line rotates `Γ` clockwise at `2β` radians
  per metre, on a circle of constant `|Γ|`. A quarter wave is half a turn, which is
  why A3's 100 Ω came back as 25 Ω. Add loss and the circle becomes a spiral inward.
  Measured: the angle turned per length, and the VSWR circle's radius, 0.3333.
- **B4 · The admittance chart.** The same picture rotated 180 degrees, because
  `Γ_y = −Γ_z`. A shunt element moves along a constant-conductance circle, and a
  series element along a constant-resistance circle. Both are needed for Group C.
  Measured: one load read on both charts, with `y = 1/z` confirmed.

### Group C: Matching networks (5)

- **C1 · The L network, in closed form.** To match 50 Ω to 100 Ω at 1.000 GHz,
  `Q = √(100/50 − 1) = 1.0000`, so `X_s = 50.00 Ω` and `X_p = 100.00 Ω`. That is
  7.9577 nH in series and 1.5915 pF in shunt. The solved network reads `Z_in = 50.000 Ω`
  exactly. Measured: both element values, and `|Γ|` below `1e-12` at 1.000 GHz.
- **C2 · Four topologies, one match.** Swapping the reactance signs and the branch
  order gives four L networks that all match at the design frequency. They differ
  away from it, and one of them is also a low-pass filter. Measured: all four give
  `|Γ| < 1e-12` at 1.000 GHz, and their responses at 2.000 GHz differ.
- **C3 · Bandwidth is the price.** The loaded Q is the same `Q` the synthesis used, so
  the fractional bandwidth is `1/Q`. The 50 Ω to 100 Ω match has `Q = 1.0000` and
  reads VSWR 1.1437 at 900 MHz. A 5 Ω to 50 Ω match has `Q = 3.0000` and 33.333 %.
  Measured: VSWR against frequency for both, and the bandwidth against `1/Q`.
- **C4 · The quarter-wave transformer.** `Z_0 = √(50 × 100) = 70.711 Ω` matches at one
  frequency and at every odd multiple of it. Its fractional bandwidth to VSWR 1.2222
  is 36.700 %, wider than C1's L network at the same transformation. Measured: the
  impedance, the bandwidth, and the repeats.
- **C5 · A complex load, absorbed.** A load of `30 − j40 Ω` is matched by cancelling
  its reactance first and then applying C1's formula to the residue. The Smith chart
  shows the two moves as two arcs. Measured: the two element values, and `|Γ|` after
  each move.

### Group D: S-parameters (5)

- **D1 · What a wave is.** At 1.000 GHz an open circuit is not open, so the two-port
  is described by what goes in and what comes back. `a` and `b` are defined from `V`
  and `I` at a reference impedance, and `S11` is `b_1/a_1` with port 2 terminated.
  Measured: `S11` of three known loads equals `Γ` from A1.
- **D2 · S from a circuit the suite can solve.** The 3.000 dB pi attenuator has
  `S11 = 0` and `S21 = 0.70795`, which is −3.0000 dB, computed from two exact AC
  solves. The same numbers come from the closed form. Measured: both entries, and
  their agreement to floating point.
- **D3 · S, Z, Y and ABCD are one object.** Convert the pi pad four ways and back.
  Each conversion is a closed form, and the round trip returns the input to `1e-12`.
  An ideal transformer has no Z-matrix, and the pane gives that reason rather than a
  large number. Measured: the round-trip error, and the singular case.
- **D4 · Cascading.** Two 3.000 dB pads give −6.0000 dB with `S11` still zero. A
  quarter-wave 70.711 Ω line between 50 Ω and 100 Ω has `|S11| = 0.33333` and
  `|S21| = 0.94281`, and `|S11|² + |S21|² = 1.000000000000`. Measured: the cascade
  both ways, and the power balance.
- **D5 · Reciprocity and loss.** A network of R, L and C has `S12 = S21`. A network of
  L and C alone has `S†S = I`, so no power is lost. Add a resistor and the sum of the
  squared magnitudes falls below one by exactly the dissipated fraction. Measured:
  the symmetry, the unitarity, and the dissipation.

### Group E: The transistor near `f_T` (5)

- **E1 · The device as four numbers.** The Electronics Lab's hybrid-π at
  `I_C = 1.00 mA` becomes an S-matrix at each frequency. `|S21|` falls at 20 dB per
  decade above the Miller-loaded pole, and reaches unity at `f_T`. Measured: the
  S-matrix against the small-signal netlist's `sweepAC`, and `f_T`.
- **E2 · Stability is not guaranteed.** Device set 1 at 2.000 GHz has `K = 0.6071`,
  so some passive load makes the amplifier oscillate. The load stability circle has
  centre `0.9347 + j0.9914` and radius 0.4997, and the pane shades the unstable
  region. Measured: `K`, `|Δ| = 0.6964`, and both circles.
- **E3 · One number instead of two.** `μ = 0.8628` for the same device, and
  `μ > 1` is a single sufficient test that `K > 1` with `|Δ| < 1` needs two of.
  Device set 2 gives `μ = 1.1314` and `K = 1.4332`. Measured: both factors on both
  devices, and their agreement about which is stable.
- **E4 · Maximum available gain.** Device set 2 reaches `MAG = 11.667 dB` at
  `Γ_S = 0.7840 ∠ −167.28°` and `Γ_L = 0.7451 ∠ 101.18°`, which are `6.13 − j5.49 Ω`
  and `12.06 + j39.64 Ω`. Device set 1 has no MAG, and the pane prints
  `MSG = 21.934 dB` with the reason. Measured: MAG, both terminations, and the MSG
  case.
- **E5 · The unilateral approximation and its error.** Setting `S12 = 0` makes the
  design two independent problems. The figure of merit `U = 0.10851` bounds the
  error between −0.895 dB and +0.998 dB, and the measured error is +0.834 dB. On
  device set 1 the bound widens to −3.844 dB and +7.067 dB, and the approximation is
  not usable. Measured: `U`, both bounds, and the exact error.

### Group F: Noise (4)

- **F1 · Noise figure defined.** `F = SNR_in/SNR_out` at a source at 290 K, where
  `k T_0 = −173.975 dBm/Hz`. A 1.5 dB amplifier adds an equivalent 119.64 K to its
  own input. The Random Signals Lab's noise group (being built) is the door to noise
  as a random process. Measured: `F`, `T_e`, and the output density.
- **F2 · A matched attenuator's noise figure is its loss.** A 3.000 dB pad at 290 K
  has `NF = 3.000 dB`, derived from the fact that its output is at 290 K whatever the
  input. Put it in front of a chain and the whole chain's NF rises by exactly 3.000 dB.
  Measured: both figures.
- **F3 · Friis, and why the first stage decides.** A 15 dB LNA at 1.5 dB, then a 10 dB
  mixer at 6 dB, then a 20 dB IF amplifier at 10 dB, give `NF = 1.8618 dB`. The LNA
  contributes 77.07 % of the excess, the mixer 17.61 % and the IF amplifier 5.32 %.
  Turn the LNA's gain up and the shares move. Measured: the total, and the three
  shares.
- **F4 · The source impedance for the lowest noise is not the one for the most
  power.** With `F_min = 0.800 dB` at `Z_opt = 56.7 + j13.6 Ω` and `R_n = 15 Ω`, a
  50 Ω source gives 0.873 dB and a 100 Ω source gives 1.116 dB. The noise circles and
  the gain circles sit on the same chart and do not share a centre. Measured: NF at
  four source impedances, and the two circle families.

### Group G: Mixers and linearity (4)

- **G1 · The mixer moves frequencies.** Multiplying 2.400 GHz by a 2.000 GHz LO gives
  400.0 MHz and 4.400 GHz, which is Signal Lab's ring modulator at another scale. An
  ideal multiplier's conversion gain is −6.0206 dB and a switching mixer's is
  −3.9224 dB. Measured: both output frequencies, and both conversion gains.
- **G2 · The image.** With the LO at 2.000 GHz, an input at 1.600 GHz lands on the
  same 400.0 MHz IF as the wanted 2.400 GHz. A quadrature mixer cancels it, and the
  cancellation is limited by imbalance. A 1 % gain error with a 3 degree phase error
  leaves 31.485 dB of rejection. Measured: the image frequency, and the rejection at
  four imbalances.
- **G3 · Two tones, and the third-order products.** Signal Lab's Nonlinearity group
  showed 100 Hz and 550 Hz appearing from 250 Hz and 400 Hz. Here the same products
  are measured in dB. At −16.021 dBm of drive the gap is 74.535 dB, so
  `OIP3 = P_out + Δ/2` gives an IIP3 of +21.247 dBm against a closed form of
  +21.249 dBm. Measured: the gap and the IIP3 at four drive levels.
- **G4 · Where the extrapolation stops working.** Drive harder and the fundamental
  compresses. At +2.041 dBm the gap is 38.098 dB and the extracted IIP3 is 0.159 dB
  low. The guard warns within 10 dB of the 1 dB compression point and declines within
  3 dB of it. Measured: the error at each level, and the guard firing at both
  thresholds.

### Group H: Oscillators and power (3)

- **H1 · The tank sets the frequency and the Q.** 10.000 nH and 2.5330 pF resonate at
  1.000 GHz with `X_L = 62.832 Ω`. At `Q = 20` the parallel resistance is 1256.6 Ω and
  the bandwidth is 50.000 MHz. The active device supplies exactly that loss. Measured:
  the frequency, both resistances, and the bandwidth.
- **H2 · Phase noise, by Leeson's model.** At `f_0 = 1.000 GHz`, `Q_L = 20`,
  `P_s = 0 dBm`, `F = 3 dB` and `f_c = 100 kHz`, the model gives −89.59 dBc/Hz at
  10 kHz and −117.00 dBc/Hz at 100 kHz. Doubling `Q_L` moves the second by 6.020 dB.
  The label beside the curve names it as a model with a fitted corner. Measured: three
  offsets, the two corners, and the `Q_L` sensitivity.
- **H3 · Efficiency against linearity.** Class A reaches 50.0 % and holds the whole
  waveform. Class B reaches `π/4`, which is 78.540 %, and cuts half of it. A 90 degree
  conduction angle reaches 94.038 %. A class A stage backed off 6.000 dB for linearity
  falls to 12.500 %. Measured: the efficiency at four conduction angles, and the
  back-off curve.

   thresholds.
7. **Oscillators and power.** `leeson.js`, the phase-noise view, the efficiency plot.
   **Group H** (3). Exit: H2's three offsets pinned and the model's label on screen.
8. **The release gate**, in order, each blocking the next. The full audit, every
   option, every preset, every claim, fuzzing, both browsers. The student sittings.
   Reed's own pass against the dark deployment. Then the flip.

Phases 1 to 3 are shippable without the Electronics Lab, the Fields Lab or the Analog
IC Lab. Phase 4 needs the Electronics Lab's `smallSignal` and Group K. Phase 5 needs
its Group O. Nothing needs the Analog IC Lab, because Decision 4 generates the device
data from a netlist rather than taking it from a chip.

## 10. Non-goals (v1, stated so they are decisions rather than omissions)

- **Electromagnetic field solving.** `EE_LABS_MAP.md` §5 keeps full-wave simulation
  out of the suite. This lab takes `Z_0` and `γ` as given and cites the Fields Lab for
  where they come from.
- **Microstrip and stripline synthesis from physical dimensions.** The closed forms
  are curve fits to numerical results, which makes them labelled data rather than
  physics. A line is described by `Z_0`, `ε_eff` and `α` here.
- **A Padé model of the line.** It would be a new labelled object under Rule 3, and no
  experiment needs one. Time-domain reflections belong to the Fields Lab's event
  engine.
- **Distributed matching with stubs on a real board.** Single-stub matching is one
  experiment's worth of content and is folded into C4's discussion rather than given a
  group. Double-stub and multi-section designs are a course this lab does not teach.
- **Filter synthesis tables.** Chebyshev and elliptic prototypes are the Applied
  Analog Lab's, and a coupled-resonator filter needs the coupling matrix that lab
  would introduce.
- **Class E and class F amplifiers.** They need the switched engine, and Power Lab's
  Group F is their home. H3 covers classes A through C.
- **Phase noise derived rather than modelled.** Leeson's form is what ships, labelled.
  A derivation from cyclostationary noise is a research topic rather than a course.
- **Antennas.** The dipole's pattern, gain and the array factor are the Fields Lab's
  antenna group. The Friis equation is used in the System Lab, not here.
- **Modulation and demodulation.** The Communications Lab's. G1 and G2 stop at the
  mixer's frequency arithmetic.
- **Vendor S-parameter files.** Decision 4 generates the device data from a netlist
  this suite solves. Nothing is loaded from a file a reader cannot check.
- **A free-form layout editor.** Curated circuits with editable values, as in every
  other lab.

## 11. Risks, named

- **The line's refusal reads as a missing feature.** A reader who has used a
  commercial simulator expects a time-domain answer. Mitigation: A5 is an experiment
  rather than a footnote, its pane states the mathematics, and the Fields Lab's
  bounce diagram is linked as the time-domain answer that does exist.
- **Two unbuilt dependencies.** The Electronics Lab and the Fields Lab both feed this
  one. Mitigation: §9 phases four groups ahead of either. Decision 4 generates the
  device data rather than waiting for the Analog IC Lab. Decision 3 gives Group A a
  term-panel fallback for `Z_0` and `γ`.
- **The Smith chart as a picture rather than a computation.** Every commercial tool
  draws a chart image with curves on top. Mitigation: `smith.js` returns centres and
  radii, invariant 5 checks every one against the algebra, and B2 is the experiment
  that shows the circles being derived.
- **Conversion conditioning.** S to Z fails near a singular sum, and a lossless
  resonance between mismatched ports makes the cascade denominator vanish.
  Mitigation: both cases throw with a reason, both are in the fuzzer's hostile corner
  list, and invariant 1 skips only the cases the singular test names.
- **The IP3 guard set at the wrong place.** Ten decibels below the 1 dB compression
  point is a choice. Mitigation: the threshold's number is derived from the measured
  extrapolation error in §4.3, the test pins that error, and the threshold moves if a
  block's measured error at it exceeds 0.1 dB.
- **Leeson's model read as physics.** It is the one object here with no exact
  counterpart. Mitigation: the label is on the curve rather than in a term panel, the
  fitted parameters are on the same pane, and the review class for labelled models
  covers it before release.
- **Numbers that are right for one device.** Every quoted gain and stability number
  is for the two sets in §4.3. Mitigation: each pin is a function of the S-matrix and
  is re-derived in the test, never typed as a constant.
- **Cost.** A new package with ten modules, two new shared canvases, and a curriculum
  that leans on two labs in flight. Mitigation: phases 1 to 3 depend on nothing
  unbuilt and are a complete course in lines, charts, matching and two-ports on their
  own.
